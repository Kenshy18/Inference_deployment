# Catmull--Rom advanced state-palette search (2026-08-24)

This experiment kept the Production curve fitter, exact CPU Recall/IoU
evaluation, topology gate, pair-vote, point refinement, and Recall floor
unchanged.  Only the DP state palette and one low-IoU loss weight were varied.

## Candidates

- `production4`: Production isotropic scales `1.000/1.005/1.025/1.035`.
- `polygon_endpoint4`: two small-scale states plus forward/backward least-square
  interval endpoint fits imported from the polygon experiments.
- `polygon_endpoint7`: endpoint fits at two horizons plus a bidirectional fit.
- `polygon_consensus4`: pose-normalized temporal median shapes.
- `curve_normal4`: Catmull--Rom interpolation points moved only along local
  curve normals toward raw-mask evidence; handles remain derived.
- `diverse7`: endpoint, consensus, and curve-normal states together.
- `polygon_endpoint4_redp`: a bounded second DP with the first refined path as
  one additional state.
- `production_endpoint6`: the complete Production palette plus two endpoint
  states.  This is the fair superset experiment.

## Findings

The initial 900-frame search showed that endpoint states were the only new
family with repeatable useful selections.  Temporal consensus and curve-normal
states often raised IoU only by inserting substantially more keyframes.  Seven
states were mostly redundant, a second DP was not stable across tracks, and
raising the low-IoU quadratic weight from 4 to 8 was effectively neutral.

On eight contiguous tracks (1,964 frames, 14--20 control points), replacing
two Production scale states with endpoint states was not competitive.  The fair
six-state superset produced the following aggregate result:

| Palette | Keys | Effective interval | Mean IoU | weighted q05 | weighted q01 | worst minimum | worst max-area ratio | DP/post-refine seconds |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Production 4 | 331 | 5.934 | 0.967734 | 0.941516 | 0.927264 | 0.859635 | 1.161321 | 29.11 |
| Production + endpoint 6 | 330 | 5.952 | 0.967941 | 0.941302 | 0.926547 | 0.857478 | 1.162325 | 54.48 |

Mean IoU and key count improved slightly, but both low tails and the worst
frame regressed, while runtime rose by 87%.

An independent six-track large-shape holdout (1,800 frames, 18--20 points)
gave:

| Palette | Keys | Effective interval | Mean IoU | weighted q05 | weighted q01 | worst minimum | worst max-area ratio | DP/post-refine seconds |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Production 4 | 480 | 3.750 | 0.963335 | 0.928096 | 0.913140 | 0.860982 | 1.137965 | 27.55 |
| Production + endpoint 6 | 474 | 3.797 | 0.962925 | 0.928681 | 0.913450 | 0.860982 | 1.138093 | 45.73 |

This is a different non-dominated point (fewer keys and better low tails, but
lower mean IoU), not a quality-preserving replacement.  One holdout track also
increased its maximum area ratio by 0.0274.  The development-set hypothesis
that endpoint states should be enabled only for 18--20-point tracks therefore
did not generalize safely.

## Decision

Do not change Production to any tested fixed palette.  The current four-state
palette remains the best robust default.  The experiment does demonstrate that
endpoint-conditioned curve shapes can improve selected tracks, but they need a
cheap per-track or per-interval acceptance gate evaluated against the
Production solution.  Without that gate, candidate diversity moves along the
Pareto frontier rather than dominating it.
