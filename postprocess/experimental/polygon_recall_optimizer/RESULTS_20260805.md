# Polygon keyframe optimizer results (2026-08-05)

## Scope and safety

- Input geometry: `12月KPI動画.sqlite`
- Baseline: interval-10 postprocess SQLite
- Evaluated class: `男性器`
- Evaluated raw-mask frames: 6,089 (frames 8,681 through 20,053)
- Reference: AI raw-mask polygon, not human-labelled semantic GT
- Privacy: video pixels were not opened, decoded, exported, or inspected
- Production pipeline and SQLite schema were not modified

All overlap metrics use continuous Shapely polygon areas. The final result is
reconstructed using the same polygon interpolation path as the overlay.

## Recall-constrained Pareto DP (current recommendation)

The new solver accepts minimum per-frame Recall as a fixed hard constraint and
constructs the non-dominated front for two objectives:

- minimize keyframe count/frequency;
- maximize mean IoU to the raw AI mask.

It keeps the best cumulative IoU for every reachable key count and removes a
state only when another state has no more keys and no lower IoU. The global
front is assembled across track segments by a second DP. This is exact within
the every-observation candidate DAG and the configured 30-frame edge horizon;
it is not a claim of continuous optimization over every possible polygon.

At fixed minimum Recall 0.97 on all 6,089 male-mask observations:

| Solution | Keys | Mean interval | Min Recall | Violations | Mean IoU |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Production interval-10 | 636 | — | 0.67933 | 1,542 | 0.87163 |
| Earlier lexicographic | 2,465 | — | 0.97014 | 0 | 0.94948 |
| Pareto minimum-key endpoint | 2,314 | 2.714 | 0.97001 | 0 | 0.96159 |
| Pareto at 2,465-key budget | 2,465 | 2.546 | 0.97000 | 0 | 0.96937 |
| Pareto normalized knee | 3,106 | 2.017 | 0.97000 | 0 | 0.97552 |
| Pareto maximum-IoU endpoint | 5,926 | 1.053 | 0.97149 | 0 | 0.97978 |

The minimum-key Pareto endpoint strictly dominates the earlier method: it uses
151 fewer keys while increasing IoU by 0.01211. At the identical 2,465-key
budget, IoU improves by 0.01989. The returned global front contains 3,613
strictly increasing key-count/IoU points.

The normalized knee independently reconstructs to exactly the DP metrics
(IoU difference `5.6e-16`, Recall difference `0`) and has mean precision
0.99384 and mean excess-area ratio 0.00613. The fixed floor therefore survives
the actual keyframe reader/interpolator path rather than only an internal
surrogate.

Optimizer time was 152.62 seconds, with 163.99 seconds wall time including
SQLite loading, independent validation, and JSON export. Peak resident memory
was 281 MB. The earlier lexicographic optimizer took 110.98 seconds, so this
reference Pareto implementation exchanges about 38% more optimizer time for a
complete front and materially better solutions. It is not yet performance
optimized.

On the longest 971-frame track, 30- and 60-frame edge horizons produced the
same solution at the 350-key comparison budget (IoU 0.9734149). The wider
horizon found a slightly lower minimum-key endpoint (290 versus 292 keys) but
doubled edge work and increased optimizer time from 26.50 to 48.85 seconds.
This supports 30 as the experimental default while preserving the horizon as
an explicit search-quality setting.

## Multiple anchor-shape states

The DP was then expanded to optimize both keyframe position and keyframe shape.
At every raw observation it can retain three geometrically distinct states:
the original highest-local-IoU feasible anchor, an alternative production/raw
projection, and a mildly expanded candidate. The exact set is selected by
geometry diversity; no candidate may violate Recall 0.97 at its anchor frame.

On all 6,089 observations, the three-state front strictly improves all 3,574
key counts shared with the one-state front:

| Comparison point | One state | Three states | Key reduction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Minimum endpoint | 2,314 keys, IoU 0.96159 | 706 keys, IoU 0.87574 | different quality |
| Reach IoU 0.96159 | 2,314 keys | 1,712 keys | 602 (26.0%) |
| Reach IoU 0.96937 | 2,465 keys | 2,193 keys | 272 (11.0%) |
| Reach IoU 0.97552 | 3,106 keys | 3,019 keys | 87 (2.8%) |
| Reach IoU 0.97978 | 5,926 keys | 5,762 keys | 164 (2.8%) |

At the same 2,314-key budget, mean IoU rises from 0.96159 to 0.97068. The
three-state normalized knee is 1,706 keys, mean interval 3.696, mean IoU
0.96146, and minimum Recall 0.97000014. It independently reconstructs with no
Recall violations. Its mean precision is 0.97682 and mean excess-area ratio is
0.02397. The knee moves because the new shape states add a large, previously
unreachable low-key portion to the front; it should not be compared to the
one-state knee as if both used identical front endpoints.

The quality gain has a substantial reference-computation cost:

| Cost | One state | Three states | Ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Candidate edges | 170,235 | 1,532,115 | 9.0x |
| Optimizer time | 152.62 s | 1,623.18 s | 10.6x |
| Full command wall time | 163.99 s | 1,639.37 s | 10.0x |
| Peak resident memory | 281 MB | 530 MB | 1.9x |

The `S^2` edge factor is fundamental to the current exact state graph: every
left shape must be paired with every right shape. The additional overhead comes
from generating and retaining state-specific fronts. Memory remained stable
during the 27-minute run. The following pass reduces wall time without changing
that total work or the resulting front.

## Exact speed optimization (2026-08-06)

The three-state algorithm and front were retained exactly while optimizing its
execution:

- cache resampled vertices, exact anchor geometry, raw areas, and bounds;
- replace 46 Python roll/reversal trials with one vectorized alignment;
- reject impossible edges using a conservative bounding-box intersection upper
  bound before invoking GEOS;
- evaluate two segments concurrently; and
- fork twelve edge workers inside each active segment on the 24-core target.

| Run | Optimizer | Command wall | Peak RSS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| One-state reference before optimization | 152.62 s | 163.99 s | 281 MB |
| Three-state initial reference | 1,623.18 s | 1,639.37 s | 530 MB |
| Three-state optimized, 2 x 12 CPU | 114.28 s | 128.34 s | 513 MB |

The optimized three-state solver is 14.20 times faster than its initial exact
implementation and 25.1% faster than the earlier one-state optimizer. Command
wall time is 21.7% lower than the one-state reference. Average CPU utilization
was 859%, with no GPU dependency.

All 5,182 Pareto points, the selected 1,706 keyframes and every polygon
coordinate are identical to the initial three-state run. The serialized front
SHA-256 is `53be0c3393e76f630f4611e3e62a818b9eef509db4386b523007cc2cb045f73e`.

Threaded GEOS evaluation was tested and rejected: 1/2/4/8 edge threads took
12.49/11.76/25.60/22.93 seconds on the 101-frame microbenchmark, with millions
of context switches. Forked edge processes took 12.19/6.90/4.14/2.78/2.39
seconds for 1/2/4/8/12 processes with an identical front.

The RTX 5090 was also evaluated as an execution option. The remaining work is
continuous polygon construction, validity repair, intersection, and area in
GEOS. CUDA/PyTorch does not provide an exact equivalent, and cuSpatial is not
installed. A GPU raster approximation would change boundary areas and could
both accept Recall violations and remove valid Pareto edges, so it was not used
for the exact optimizer.

## Earlier result: lexicographic optimizer

The second experiment removes fixed key spacing entirely:

1. start each track segment from its endpoints;
2. split the worst reconstructed span until dense recall is feasible;
3. remove redundant keys;
4. smooth key motion while projecting every key back to its recall constraint;
5. re-check every frame and split only any newly failing spans.

The priorities are lexicographic rather than a weighted average: recall safety,
key count, excess area/IoU, then stability. Final scale repair is disabled, so
the method cannot hide a miss by dilating the whole interval.

| Metric | Production | Lexicographic |
|---|---:|---:|
| Keyframes | 636 | 606 (-4.7%) |
| Minimum recall | 0.6793 | 0.9006 |
| Frames below 0.90 recall | 116 | 0 |
| Keyframe minimum recall | 0.6793 | 0.9700 |
| Mean IoU | 0.8716 | 0.8730 |
| IoU q05 | 0.7021 | 0.7291 |
| Mean precision | 0.8908 | 0.9035 |
| Precision q05 | 0.7264 | 0.7531 |
| Mean excess-area ratio | 0.1349 | 0.1153 |
| Excess-area q95 | 0.3646 | 0.3179 |
| Mean centroid error (px) | 14.316 | 13.770 |
| Adjacent-mask IoU | 0.95283 | 0.95343 |
| Mean absolute area log-delta | 0.01249 | 0.01224 |
| Mean centroid acceleration (px) | 1.3609 | 1.2970 |
| Mean centroid-residual acceleration (px) | 6.723 | 6.081 |
| Centroid-residual acceleration q95 (px) | 24.122 | 21.385 |

This solution uses 30 fewer keys and improves all listed quality and mean
stability metrics while eliminating the recall-floor violations. Mean recall
falls from 0.9760 to 0.9636 by design: surplus coverage above the 0.90 safety
floor is exchanged for higher precision and less over-mask.

Two stability tails do not dominate production. Absolute centroid-acceleration
q95 rises from 6.05 to 7.16 px and area-log-delta q95 from 0.0413 to 0.0438.
Absolute acceleration penalizes genuine rapid motion as well as flicker, so the
motion-adjusted centroid-residual metric was added; its q95 improves by 11.3%.
Relative-area flicker is 2.4% worse and remains a target for further work.

For the nine frames identified in the editor feedback, minimum recall improves
from 0.7923 to 0.9102 or better. Eight improve substantially; frame 14,424
improves only from 0.9081 to 0.9102 but already satisfies the floor.

## Strict same-key-count comparison

The strongest robust same-budget candidate keeps the production key positions,
blends each key shape 50% toward its raw observation, and applies interval-local
scale repair capped at 1.25. Both rows contain exactly 636 in-range keys.

| Metric | Production | Experimental, same 636 keys |
|---|---:|---:|
| Minimum recall | 0.6793 | 0.7906 |
| Frames below 0.90 recall | 116 | 12 |
| Keyframes below 0.90 recall | 31 | 0 |
| Mean IoU | 0.8716 | 0.8786 |
| Mean precision | 0.8908 | 0.8996 |
| Mean excess-area ratio | 0.1349 | 0.1352 |
| 95th-percentile excess-area ratio | 0.3646 | 0.5570 |
| Predicted adjacent IoU | 0.9528 | 0.9541 |
| Mean absolute area log-delta | 0.01249 | 0.01266 |
| Mean centroid acceleration (px) | 1.3609 | 1.2427 |

This removes 89.7% of the recall-floor violations without increasing keyframe
frequency and improves mean IoU, adjacent IoU, and centroid smoothness. It does
not dominate production: 12 difficult frames remain, the area-change metric is
1.4% worse, and the over-mask tail is substantially larger. A fixed budget is
therefore a preference, not a valid hard safety constraint.

Greedy wholesale replacement of key positions with worst-recall raw frames did
not generalize. On the full range it produced 634 violations before repair and
40 after scale-1.25 repair. Raw anchors, unconstrained pair-vote, fixed maximum
gaps, and global dilation also failed to dominate the production result.

## Adaptive recall-floor results

The adaptive method begins with the conservative 50% anchor blend, measures
every reconstructed frame, repairs each interval within an expansion cap, and
adds the single worst violating frame in each failing interpolation span. It
stops only after the dense minimum recall passes 0.90.

| Max linear scale | Keys | Added | Min recall | Violations | Mean IoU | Mean precision | Mean excess | Excess q95 | Adjacent IoU |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.10 | 669 | +33 (+5.19%) | 0.9007 | 0 | 0.8926 | 0.9147 | 0.1042 | 0.3547 | 0.9518 |
| 1.15 | 655 | +19 (+2.99%) | 0.9071 | 0 | 0.8865 | 0.9080 | 0.1158 | 0.4378 | 0.9527 |
| 1.20 | 640 | +4 (+0.63%) | 0.9006 | 0 | 0.8808 | 0.9018 | 0.1286 | 0.5233 | 0.9538 |
| 1.25 | 639 | +3 (+0.47%) | 0.9081 | 0 | 0.8804 | 0.9014 | 0.1295 | 0.5303 | 0.9538 |

At scale 1.25, only three keys are added across 6,089 mask frames. Compared
with production, all 116 violations disappear, minimum recall rises by 0.2287,
mean IoU rises by 0.0088, mean precision rises by 0.0106, mean excess area falls
by 0.0054, adjacent IoU improves slightly, and centroid acceleration falls from
1.3609 px to 1.2463 px. Its cost is a larger over-mask tail: excess-area q95
rises from 0.3646 to 0.5303.

At scale 1.10, the method uses 33 additional keys but yields the best overall
mask agreement and the least mean over-mask. This is the current quality-first
candidate. Scale 1.20 is the balanced candidate; scale 1.25 is the
minimum-edit-frequency candidate.

## Interpretation

The data supports a lexicographic objective rather than a weighted average:

1. enforce dense per-frame recall as a hard constraint;
2. minimize added keyframes and excess area on the feasible set;
3. use IoU and temporal stability to choose among feasible alternatives.

Average recall alone is unsafe. It hides short severe misses. Fixed keyframe
interval is also the wrong primary target: some spans need no new key, while a
fast excursion or rotation needs an immediate key. Expansion and added keys
form a measurable Pareto frontier and should be the user-facing tradeoff.

The experiment is promising but is not production-ready yet. Human-GT mask
evaluation, additional videos/classes, raster-equivalence checks, and a review
of the 95th-percentile excess-area tail are required before integration.
